About Me

I'm Natalia, a Physics and Computer Science graduate from the University of Toronto with experience spanning applied AI, scientific programming, and experimental condensed-matter physics. At IBM AI & Automation, I worked on multi-agent orchestration across cloud platforms and on agentic workflows for the software-development lifecycle. In the Julian Lab at U of T, I designed, built, debugged, and characterized a cryogenic tunnel-diode oscillator for contactless resistivity measurements. My work is driven by a common theme: using computation to make complex technical systems useful, measurable, and easier to understand.

Research

Experimental condensed-matter research in Professor Stephen Julian's laboratory at the University of Toronto, focused on cryogenic instrumentation for contactless measurements of superconducting materials.

A ~23 MHz Tunnel Diode Oscillator for Cryogenic Resistivity Measurements

Designed, constructed, and characterized a first-generation cryogenic TDO. The system achieved stable self-oscillation near 23.1 MHz and showed an average ~97 kHz upward frequency shift on cooling to 77 K.
